# Blood Cipher

## The idea

The cult writes in blood because it is **visible, personal, and ambiguous**. The player must decide what the writing says, what it hides, and whether it was written by the victim or the killer.

Blood ciphers are the most story-heavy puzzle family. They carry the victim's last message, the killer's signature, or a false trail.

## Surfaces

| Surface | Where | What the player does |
|---|---|---|
| **Mirror writing** | Mirror, window, polished silver | Read the reversed text |
| **Substitution** | Ledger, letter, wall | Replace symbols with letters using a key |
| **UV hidden** | Wall, rug, body, altar | Reveal the second layer with the UV torch |
| **Smeared text** | Floor, door, bathtub | Reconstruct the missing letters |
| **Blood trail** | Corridor, stairs, cellar | Follow the droplets to a room or sigil |

## Example — easy

A mirror in the guest room has a message written in blood. It reads backwards:

```text
EHT DLROW SI DLO
```

Read in the mirror: **THE WORLD IS OLD**. The phrase is a hint to the archive's oldest book.

## Example — medium

A blood smear on the chapel wall has letters missing where the blood ran. The player uses the UV torch to reveal the dried edges and reconstructs:

```text
_ H E _ O L L O W _ O R D
```

**THE HOLLOW WORD**. The missing letters are the first three letters of the Hollow Word.

## Example — hard

A ledger page has a substitution cipher. The key is the victim's monogram, repeated around the page border. The decrypted text is the motive:

> "He paid for the silence. The silence paid him back."

## Solution logic

1. **Identify the medium.** Mirror, UV, substitution, smear, or trail.
2. **Find the key.** A mirror, a monogram, a codebook, or the UV torch.
3. **Decode.** Reverse, substitute, fill gaps, or follow.
4. **Interpret.** The decoded text is a clue, a name, a number, or a warning.

## Item synergy

| Item | Effect |
|---|---|
| **UV Torch** | Reveals hidden layers, dried edges, and invisible ink. |
| **Magnifying Glass** | Reads small text and blood spatter direction. |
| **Camera** | Captures the writing for later comparison. |
| **Notebook** | Tracks substitutions and missing letters. |

## Integration

- **Clue:** A blood message names the victim's contact or the weapon.
- **Sigil:** The sigil is drawn in blood; the player must read the mirrored word and speak it (or type it) to break it.
- **Seal:** The third-floor seal is a substitution cipher on a chapel door.

## Content rules

- **Stylized blood only.** Dark red-brown stains, no gore, no splatter on faces.
- **No self-harm imagery.** The blood is from the victim, not the player.
- **No real-world hate symbols.** The cipher alphabet is fictional.
- **Readable in blackout.** UV mode is always available if the player has the torch; otherwise the text glows faintly.

## Accessibility

- All blood text has a high-contrast mode.
- The mirror puzzle has a "flip" button in text mode.
- The UV layer is available as a text description for screen readers.
- Colorblind players can use the "dried edge" outline instead of color.

## Design notes

- Blood ciphers should be **short**. One sentence, one name, one number.
- The handwriting should differ between victim and killer. That difference is a clue.
- The killer can plant a blood message, but it must be visually distinguishable to an expert (the item clue).
- The puzzle should never require the player to read cursive.
