# Zodiac Puzzle

## The idea

The lodge is full of **old money mysticism**. The zodiac is the polite occult: acceptable in a drawing room, deniable at a dinner party. The cult uses it because it looks like decoration.

The puzzle maps **twelve symbols** to numbers, dates, elements, or directions. The player finds a marked chart, a date, or a phrase, then reads the corresponding symbol.

## Surfaces

| Surface | Where | What the player does |
|---|---|---|
| **Star chart** | Study, archive, attic | Find the three marked constellations; read their house numbers |
| **Zodiac wheel** | Chapel door, cabinet, desk | Rotate the inner ring to align a date with a symbol |
| **Almanac** | Library, guest room | Match a date to a sign, then to an element |
| **Ceiling fresco** | Ballroom | Read the order of the signs around the room |
| **Pocket watch** | On a body or in a drawer | The engraved sign gives the hour |

## Example — easy

A star chart on the study wall has three constellations circled in red: **Taurus**, **Leo**, **Pisces**.

The almanac on the desk maps signs to numbers:

```text
Aries 1   Taurus 2   Gemini 3   Cancer 4
Leo 5     Virgo 6    Libra 7    Scorpio 8
Sagittarius 9   Capricorn 10   Aquarius 11   Pisces 12
```

The numbers are **2–5–12**. The desk drawer has a three-digit lock. Open it to find the item clue.

## Example — medium

A chapel door has a brass wheel with the twelve signs. The inscription reads:

> "The Ram opens at the hour of the Bull."

The player sets the wheel to **Aries** (the Ram) and the hour hand to **Taurus** (the second sign, or 2 o'clock). The door opens.

## Example — hard

The ballroom ceiling has the signs in a circle, but two are missing. The guest list gives the missing guests' birth dates. The player must infer the missing signs, then read the order of the remaining ten as a number sequence. The sequence opens the second-pistol seal on the first floor.

## Solution logic

1. **Identify the anchor.** A date, a name, a phrase, or a marked symbol.
2. **Map the anchor to a sign.** Use the almanac, the wheel, or the ceiling order.
3. **Convert the sign to a value.** Number, element (fire/earth/air/water), direction, or letter.
4. **Apply the value.** Enter the code, align the wheel, or choose the next symbol.

## Item synergy

| Item | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Notebook** | Records the chart and auto-marks the signs the player has seen. |
| **Compass** | Shows the direction associated with each sign in exterior puzzles. |
| **Camera** | Takes a photo of the chart; the photo can be studied later. |
| **Magnifying Glass** | Reveals the tiny engraved dates on the wheel. |

## Integration

- **Clue:** A zodiac chart hides the combination to a desk or safe.
- **Sigil:** A sigil has a ring of signs; the player must align the sign that matches the victim's birth date.
- **Seal:** The first-floor seal is a zodiac wheel with a missing sign.

## Accessibility

- The wheel has a text mode: a list of signs and a numeric input.
- The star chart has a high-contrast mode that outlines the constellations.
- The almanac is readable by screen reader.
- No puzzle requires knowing real astrology; all mappings are printed in the room.

## Design notes

- The zodiac is **decorative occultism**, not a real belief system. Do not use it to make claims about real people.
- The signs should feel like old bookplates, not neon.
- The puzzle should be solvable by a player who has never opened an almanac.
- The answer should always be a short code (3–5 digits) or a single symbol.
