# Case File Questions

> [!info] Four tasks, three options each
> The specific answers are defined by the scenario card. This page defines the **shape** of each task and gives examples. Do not treat the examples as canon for every match.

## Task design contract

Every task must:

- Ask a question a detective would actually ask.
- Have **three plausible options**.
- Have a **free clue** that eliminates one wrong option.
- Have an **item clue** that confirms the correct option.
- Tie to a **story beat** (the victim, the method, the scene, the motive).
- Be solvable without voice.
- Be solvable in blackout with the right item.
- Have a **red herring** the killer can exploit.
- Award exactly **one letter** of the Hollow Word.

## Task 1 — The Victim

**Question:** Who was the victim?

**What the player is solving:** Identity, not just a name. The victim's profession, connection to the lodge, and reason for being invited.

**Three options (example):**
- A. **Arthur Vale**, the industrialist who funded the 1926 expedition.
- B. **Marguerite Vale**, the journalist investigating the expedition.
- C. **Julian Rook**, the heir who inherited the Vale fortune.

**Free clue:** A scorched invitation in the fireplace. The visible half reads "...VALE" and a partial title. It eliminates one option whose name does not fit the visible letters.

**Item clue:** The **Camera** or **Notebook** reveals the full guest list and the victim's portrait in the foyer. The portrait's plaque gives the full name and profession.

**Definitive answer:** The scenario card's victim.

**Story meaning:** The victim is not a random guest. They are a **debt** the cult is collecting.

**Red herring:** A monogrammed coat in the cloakroom with the wrong initials. The killer can move it to support the wrong option.

**Killer's angle:** The killer knows the victim. They can "recognize" the body and steer the team toward the wrong identity.

## Task 2 — The Method

**Question:** How were they killed?

**What the player is solving:** The murder weapon and cause of death, not the staging.

**Three options (example):**
- A. **A letter opener** from the study.
- B. **A bell cord** from the chapel.
- C. **Laudanum** from the medicine cabinet.

**Free clue:** A bloody letter opener beside the body. The blood is smeared, not spattered. It eliminates one option that would produce a different blood pattern.

**Item clue:** The **Magnifying Glass** reveals the wound's shape and the absence of a matching edge on the letter opener. The wound is a thin, triangular puncture: an ice pick, not a letter opener.

**Definitive answer:** The scenario card's method.

**Story meaning:** The visible weapon is a **prop**. The real weapon is missing because the killer took it.

**Red herring:** The letter opener is the obvious answer. It is always wrong, or always a plant. The scenario card can vary which obvious weapon is the plant.

**Killer's angle:** The killer places the plant weapon. They can also hide the real weapon in a room the team has already searched.

## Task 3 — The Scene

**Question:** Where and how was the scene staged?

**What the player is solving:** The location of the murder and the staging pattern, including the killer's signature.

**Three options (example):**
- A. **The study**, staged as a suicide.
- B. **The chapel**, staged as a ritual.
- C. **The ballroom**, staged as a party that never ended.

**Free clue:** A strange smell in the room: candle wax, incense, or perfume. It eliminates one option whose room does not match the smell.

**Item clue:** The **UV Torch** reveals a blood trail and a sigil painted under the rug. The trail leads to the true staging room. The **Camera** can capture the sigil for the board.

**Definitive answer:** The scenario card's staging room and signature.

**Story meaning:** The scene is a **story the killer wants the investigators to believe**. The signature is the one true thing in it.

**Red herring:** A second staged room with a weaker version of the same tableau. The killer can set it up to split the team.

**Killer's angle:** The killer chooses the staging room during the shop. They can place the red herring in a room that contradicts the free clue.

## Task 4 — The Motive

**Question:** Why did the cult want them dead?

**What the player is solving:** The cult's reason, which exposes the connection between the victim and the lodge.

**Three options (example):**
- A. **Silence.** The victim was going to expose the 1926 expedition.
- B. **Money.** The victim's will leaves the lodge to a foundation that does not exist.
- C. **Revenge.** The victim was one of the nine guests who survived.

**Free clue:** A torn ledger page with a circled debt. It eliminates one option that does not fit the debt.

**Item clue:** The **Cipher Wheel** or **Recorder** decodes a letter from the victim to Elias. The letter threatens to publish the expedition's findings. The motive is silence.

**Definitive answer:** The scenario card's motive.

**Story meaning:** The cult kills to keep a story from being told. The investigators are the next story.

**Red herring:** A forged will that points to money. The killer can plant it as their one false clue.

**Killer's angle:** The killer knows the true motive. They can argue for the red herring with complete sincerity.

## The ritual slot

The fifth slot is not a question. It is the **Revealing Ritual**. It does not have options, a free clue, or an item clue. It has:

- A site (the chapel).
- A channel time (30 seconds).
- An interrupt window.
- A reveal (all killers).
- A letter (the fifth).

See [[Ritual Overview]].

## Task order and pacing

The tasks are designed to be solved in **any order**, but the intended pacing is:

| Match time | Task | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 6:00–10:00 | Victim | Easiest; teaches the loop |
| 10:00–14:00 | Method | Requires a specific item; first pistol at 1/4 |
| 14:00–20:00 | Scene | Post-shift; the map has changed |
| 20:00–24:00 | Motive | Deepest story; sets up the ritual |
| 24:00–28:00 | Ritual | Endgame |

The team can rush the ritual with zero tasks. That is a valid but desperate strategy: they get the reveal and the fifth letter, but no case reward and no letters 1–4.

## Difficulty knobs

Per task, the scenario card can tune:

- **Free clue clarity:** obvious vs. cryptic.
- **Item rarity:** common vs. hidden.
- **Option similarity:** distinct vs. near-synonyms.
- **Red herring strength:** weak vs. convincing.
- **Room distance:** near the body vs. across the map.

The global difficulty target is **medium**. See [[Difficulty and Pacing]].

## Text-only and accessibility

- Every question and option is readable in the hotkey UI.
- The free clue has a text description, not just a model.
- The item clue has a text description and an image.
- The answer buttons are keyboard/gamepad navigable.
- The 60-second lock has a visible timer and an audio cue.
- The letter animation has a text equivalent.
