# Design Principles

These are practical rules for anyone building a feature. They sit under the pillars and above the systems.

## 1. Every feature must produce evidence, pressure, or movement

If a feature does not help someone find a clue, apply pressure, or move through the house, it is probably decoration. Decoration is fine, but it is not a mechanic.

## 2. The killer lies with actions, not just words

The best killer gameplay is staging, marking, planting, and misdirecting. A killer who only talks is a killer who can be muted. Give the killer physical decisions that the investigators can read.

## 3. The free clue must be useful, not a tease

The free clue should eliminate one wrong option or point to a room. It must never be pure flavor. The player should feel smart for finding it, even without the item.

## 4. The item clue must be definitive

If a player spends Shillings on the matching item, the answer should be certain. Do not add another dice roll after the purchase.

## 5. No soft-locks

Every task, the chapel, the vault, and the exit must be reachable from every valid seed. The killer cannot destroy a true clue. A disconnected player's items must drop.

## 6. Randomness must be readable

A procedural room should still make sense: a kitchen next to a pantry, a chapel with two entrances, a cellar under the kitchen. Randomize placement, not logic.

## 7. Information asymmetry must be counterable

Every killer advantage needs a counter: the mark can be broken, the false clue can be exposed, the ritual can be defended, the second pistol can be contested.

## 8. Death is a spectator experience, not a punishment

A dead player should have something to watch, a way to talk to other dead players, and a reason to stay for the results. No pay-to-revive.

## 9. Voice is a bonus, never a requirement

Every mechanic must work with text and pings. A text-only player should be able to solve the case and vote.

## 10. The 30-minute match is sacred

No feature may add a long downtime, a meeting, a cutscene, or a grind. If a feature adds more than 60 seconds of waiting, redesign it.

## 11. Readability beats realism

A player should understand a room, a clue, and a threat at a glance. Exaggerate silhouettes, light the important props, and signpost the exits.

## 12. No pay-to-win

Cosmetics only. The match is decided by knowledge, teamwork, and nerve, not by Robux.

## Bonus: the "one more match" test

After a match, ask: "Do I want to play one more?" If the answer is no because the match was too long, too confusing, or too unfair, fix that before adding content.

## Bonus: the "tell a friend" test

Can a player describe what happened in one sentence? *"The killer interrupted the ritual, shot the wrong guy, and escaped at dawn."* If the match produces no story, the systems are too quiet.
