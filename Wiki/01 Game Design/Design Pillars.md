# Design Pillars

A pillar is not a feature. It is a rule that decides which features are allowed. If a proposed feature violates a pillar, the feature loses.

## 1. Paranoia without meetings

**Means:** The social pressure comes from movement, proximity, and evidence, not from a round-table vote.

**Forbids:** Emergency meetings, long discussion timers, a safe "everyone stop and talk" phase, and mechanics that only work if all players stand still.

**Test:** Can a player lie, accuse, or deflect while walking, searching, or running? If not, the feature is too static.

## 2. Evidence over accusation

**Means:** The Case File is solved by finding and interpreting physical clues. Accusation alone does not win.

**Forbids:** A pure popularity vote that ignores the case, clues that are only flavor, and answers that can be brute-forced without evidence.

**Test:** Can a team that never explored the house still win by guessing? If yes, the evidence loop is too weak.

## 3. The house is an antagonist

**Means:** The map changes, locks, shifts, and hides. Navigation is part of the mystery.

**Forbids:** A fixed, memorizable layout; a minimap that reveals everything; rooms that exist only as decoration.

**Test:** Does a veteran player still have to look, listen, and adapt? If not, the house is too predictable.

## 4. Every session tells a different crime

**Means:** Scenario cards, killer staging, procedural rooms, and randomized clues combine into a fresh case.

**Forbids:** A single canonical crime, fixed clue locations, and a killer whose staging choices do not matter.

**Test:** Can two matches with the same scenario card feel different? If not, the generation is too shallow.

## 5. Items change what you can know

**Means:** The shop is about information and access, not raw power. The right item turns a vague clue into a fact.

**Forbids:** Cosmetic-only shop items, pay-to-win tools, and items that are always strictly better than others.

**Test:** Is there a real trade-off between buying an item, saving Shillings, and covering another task? If not, the economy is flat.

## 6. Death is not the end

**Means:** Dead players become spectators with dead-only chat. They stay in the story without influencing the living.

**Forbids:** Instant disconnect, pay-to-revive, and dead players talking to the living.

**Test:** Would a dead player stay to watch the endgame? If not, the spectator experience is too empty.

## 7. Fair information asymmetry

**Means:** The killer has secret knowledge, but the investigators have tools, numbers, and the ability to verify. Both sides can make informed plays.

**Forbids:** Uncounterable killer abilities, clues that can be permanently destroyed, and a killer who can soft-lock the case.

**Test:** Can the investigators explain why they lost in terms of decisions, not just bad luck? If not, the asymmetry is unfair.

## 8. Noir, not gore

**Means:** Rain, shadows, trenches, typewriters, old money, and dread. Violence is stylized and implied.

**Forbids:** Excessive gore, torture imagery, and shock for shock's sake.

**Test:** Would the scene work in black and white with a saxophone? If not, the tone is off.

## Pillar conflict resolution

When two pillars conflict, use this order:

1. Fair information asymmetry (the game must be winnable).
2. Paranoia without meetings (the core fantasy).
3. Evidence over accusation (the core activity).
4. Every session tells a different crime (replayability).
5. The house is an antagonist (identity).
6. Items change what you can know (depth).
7. Death is not the end (retention).
8. Noir, not gore (tone).

## How to use this page

- In a design review, name the pillar a proposal serves.
- If a proposal violates a pillar, write down which one and why.
- If a pillar needs to change, record a decision in [[Decision Log]]; do not change it in a feature page.
