# Puzzles

Puzzles appear in four places:

1. **Clues** — decode a message to find a task answer.
2. **Sigils** — break the sigil before the countdown ends.
3. **Seals** — open the second-pistol cabinet.
4. **The exit** — enter the Hollow Word at the gate.

There are **five puzzle families**. Every puzzle is generated from a template, so the solution changes each match.

## 1. Zodiac

**What it looks like:** a ring of symbols, a date, a constellation, or a name written in the zodiac.

**How to solve:**
- Find the key: a birth date, a plaque, a guest's star sign, or a note.
- Match the symbol to the sign.
- Read the sequence in the order the clue gives you (clockwise, oldest to newest, or by house number).

**Items that help:** Cipher Wheel, Notebook, Camera.

**Tip:** The zodiac ring is often a **date**, not a code. Look for a date nearby.

## 2. Binary

**What it looks like:** rows of 0s and 1s, a broken clock, a switch panel, or a pattern of lit windows.

**How to solve:**
- Group the digits into **8-bit** chunks.
- Convert each chunk to a letter using the ASCII table in the Case File.
- If the chunks are 5-bit, use the A1Z26 table instead.

**Items that help:** Notebook, Cipher Wheel, Recorder.

**Tip:** The number of digits tells you the grouping. Do not force 8-bit if the rows are five long.

## 3. Blood cipher

**What it looks like:** writing on a wall, a mirror, or a letter, written in blood or UV ink.

**How to solve:**
- Read it in a mirror, under UV light, or through a colored filter.
- The cipher may be a **Caesar shift**, a **substitution**, or a **reverse alphabet**.
- The key is usually in the room: a name, a number, or a phrase.

**Items that help:** UV Torch, Cipher Wheel, Camera.

**Tip:** Blood writing often has a **second layer**. Check the wall behind the writing.

## 4. Rune alignment

**What it looks like:** a set of rotating rings, standing stones, or a sigil with missing pieces.

**How to solve:**
- Rotate the rings so the runes line up with the marks on the frame.
- The correct alignment usually forms a **word** or a **shape**.
- Some puzzles require you to align the runes to a **date** or a **constellation**.

**Items that help:** Compass, Notebook, UV Torch.

**Tip:** Start with the rune that has a unique shape. It usually has only one valid position.

## 5. Knot and pattern

**What it looks like:** a woven knot, a carpet pattern, a bell rope, or a sequence of symbols.

**How to solve:**
- Trace the pattern from the marked starting point.
- The knot may encode a **number**, a **name**, or a **direction**.
- Count the crossings, loops, or repeated symbols.

**Items that help:** Magnifying Glass, Camera, Notebook.

**Tip:** The pattern is often symmetrical. Find the axis first.

## Where puzzles appear

| Context | Difficulty | Time pressure |
|---|---|---|
| **Clue** | Easy–Medium | None; you can come back |
| **Sigil** | Medium | 90 seconds (extendable in accessibility) |
| **Seal** | Hard | None, but the killer is hunting |
| **Exit** | Easy | Enter the word you already earned |

## Hints

You can get hints from:

- **The room:** a plaque, a date, a name, a painting.
- **Your items:** the Cipher Wheel, Notebook, UV Torch, and Compass each reveal a layer.
- **The Case File:** some puzzles use the same symbols as the task cards.
- **Your team:** voice or text. A second pair of eyes is the best hint.
- **The hint button:** after 60 seconds on a sigil, you can request a hint. It costs Cinders but does not fail the puzzle.

## Accessibility

- **Text mode:** every puzzle has a text description and a readable input.
- **Colorblind palettes:** no puzzle relies on color alone.
- **Extended timer:** +50% puzzle time and +15 seconds on sigils.
- **Screen reader:** puzzle prompts and inputs are labeled.
- **One-handed mode:** all puzzle interactions can be done with one hand.

See [[07 Player Wiki/Accessibility|Accessibility]].

## Etiquette

- **Do not spoil solutions** in public chat.
- **Do not camp a puzzle** to prevent someone else from solving it.
- **Do not fake a puzzle failure** to get someone killed. That is griefing.
- **Share the method, not the answer.** Teach the puzzle; do not solve it for them every time.
