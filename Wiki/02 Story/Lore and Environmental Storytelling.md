# Lore and Environmental Storytelling

> [!spoiler] Design spoiler
> This page explains where the story is hidden. It is for designers, artists, and level builders.

## The rule

**The house tells the story; the characters do not.**

No NPC should explain the plot in a paragraph. The player should be able to reconstruct the truth from:

- What is on the walls.
- What is missing from the rooms.
- What the staff will not say.
- What the house changes at midnight.
- What the Case File makes them look at twice.

The story has three layers. Every room should carry at least one layer, and the best rooms carry all three.

| Layer | What the player sees | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Surface** | A rich 1940s lodge in a storm. Old money, dark wood, good liquor. | Comfort. Safety. The horror is off-screen. |
| **Middle** | Cracks in the luxury: a locked chapel, a burned ledger, a guest list with nine names. | The cult. The cover-up. The cold case. |
| **Deep** | Architecture that does not make sense; rooms that move; a word that opens a door. | The Hollow. The thing under the house. |

## The five storytelling tools

### 1. Props with a history

Every prop should answer one of three questions:

- **Who was here?** A cigarette case, a coat, a letter, a glass with lipstick.
- **What happened?** A broken watch stopped at 11:40, a torn dress, a bullet hole behind a painting.
- **What was hidden?** A false-bottom drawer, a ledger with pages cut out, a portrait with the eyes scratched out.

Props should not be random clutter. If a prop is interactive, it should either carry a clue, support a red herring, or reward attention with a story beat.

### 2. Rooms as arguments

Each room makes an argument about the house:

| Room | The argument | The tell |
|---|---|---|
| **Foyer** | "Welcome. You are expected." | The guest book has ten lines, not nine. |
| **Ballroom** | "We were happy here once." | The piano is in tune; no one has tuned it in ten years. |
| **Study** | "The case was solved." | The file is complete except for the autopsy photo. |
| **Chapel** | "This is a place of worship." | The altar faces the wrong direction. |
| **Cellar** | "Nothing is buried here." | The floor is newer than the walls. |
| **Archive** | "The truth is on paper." | The 1926 map is filed under 1936. |
| **Kitchen** | "Someone still cooks here." | Ten place settings, one chair pushed in. |
| **Attic** | "The house remembers." | A child's toy from a guest who had no children. |

### 3. Sound as evidence

The audio layer should be **information**, not just atmosphere:

- **Rain** masks footsteps. When the rain stops, the house is too quiet.
- **Thunder** covers the sound of a door opening. Players learn to move on the thunder.
- **The clock** in the foyer is always wrong by ten minutes. The Case File's "time of death" question depends on it.
- **The piano** plays one note when the chapel is opened.
- **Whispers** follow the killer's mark; they are directional if the player has headphones.
- **The typewriter** in the study types by itself during the ritual.

### 4. Light as a map

Lighting is not only mood. It tells the player where to go and what is safe:

- **Warm practicals** (lamps, candles, fireplaces) mark rooms with clues.
- **Cold moonlight** marks exterior sightlines and escape routes.
- **Red light** marks ritual spaces and the killer's sabotage.
- **Blackouts** force players to use items, memory, and voice.
- **The chapel** is the only room with no shadows. That is not comforting.

### 5. UI as a document

The Case File, evidence board, and shop are diegetic documents:

- The Case File looks like a **typewritten police file**, not a game menu.
- The evidence board is a **corkboard in the foyer**, with string, pins, and photographs.
- The shop is a **curio cabinet** owned by Vesper; the UI is the cabinet's drawers.
- The Hollow Word is entered on a **brass letter lock** at the gate.
- The ending card is a **closed case file** with a stamp.

## The story in the four tasks

Each Case File task should have environmental support:

### Task 1 — Victim

**Question:** Who was the victim?

**Environmental support:**
- A portrait in the foyer with the victim's face cut out.
- A guest list with the victim's name in a different hand.
- A coat in the cloakroom with a monogram that does not match the victim's initials.
- A newspaper clipping about the victim's company.

**The lie:** The victim was invited as a guest. The truth is they were invited as a **payment**.

### Task 2 — Method

**Question:** How were they killed?

**Environmental support:**
- The weapon is placed in the room, but it is not the murder weapon.
- A wound on the body that does not match the obvious weapon.
- A missing item from the study: a letter opener, a candlestick, a surgical knife.
- Blood on a cuff that is not the victim's.

**The lie:** The visible weapon. The truth is the **absence** of the right one.

### Task 3 — Scene

**Question:** Where and how was the scene staged?

**Environmental support:**
- The body is arranged in a tableau: a chair, a book, a candle, a sigil.
- The room is too clean in one place and too dirty in another.
- A rug pulled back, a painting straightened, a clock reset.
- The signature is a repeated gesture: a coin on the eyes, a flower in the mouth, a word on the wall.

**The lie:** The room looks like the murder scene. The truth is it is a **stage**.

### Task 4 — Motive

**Question:** Why did the cult want them dead?

**Environmental support:**
- A ledger with a debt circled.
- A photograph of the victim with Elias, ten years younger.
- A letter from the victim threatening to expose the expedition.
- A will that leaves everything to a foundation that does not exist.

**The lie:** Money. The truth is **silence**.

## The midnight shift

The shift is the house's biggest storytelling move. When the clock strikes midnight:

- One wing reconfigures. A corridor is longer. A door is where a wall was.
- A room that was locked is now open. A room that was open is now sealed.
- The portrait in the foyer changes: the victim is in it now.
- The guest book has a new line, written in the victim's hand.
- The chapel bell rings once, though the bell was removed in 1936.

The shift should make the player feel that the house is **correcting the story**. It is not random. It is the house making the version it prefers.

## The staff as unreliable narrators

Each staff member knows one true thing and one lie:

| Staff | True thing | Lie |
|---|---|---|
| **Mr. Finch** (butler) | He saw the killer leave the study. | He says the study was locked all night. |
| **Ada** (maid) | She cleaned the chapel after the 1936 rite. | She says she was hired in 1940. |
| **The Cook** | The cellar has a door that is not on the plans. | She says the cellar floods every spring. |
| **Silas** (groundskeeper) | The woods have a path that only appears in the rain. | He says he never goes into the woods. |
| **Rook** (chauffeur) | He drove the victim here. | He says the victim arrived by train. |

The staff are not quest givers. They are **witnesses** the player can choose to believe.

## The player wiki boundary

The player wiki may describe:

- The lodge's rooms and atmosphere.
- The staff's names and public roles.
- The existence of the cult, without its doctrine.
- The Case File tasks, without answers.
- The five puzzle families, without solutions.
- The fact that the map changes, without the seed.

The player wiki may not describe:

- The nine guests' identities and fates.
- Elias's role as the survivor.
- Vesper's defection.
- The killer's staging options.
- The scenario cards.
- The Hollow Word list.
- The ending matrix.

## Content boundaries

- **Stylized blood, no gore.** Blood is a dark stain, a red ribbon, a smear on a wall. No viscera, no dismemberment, no photorealistic wounds.
- **No real-world hate symbols.** The cult's iconography is fictional: antlers, knots, a hollow circle, a five-letter word.
- **No sexual content.** The noir tone is adult, but the game is not.
- **No harm to children.** The child's toy in the attic is a ghost story, not a depiction.
- **Mental illness is not the monster.** The cult is a choice, not a diagnosis.
- **No jump scares that punish accessibility.** Startle effects are optional and can be reduced.

## Room design checklist

Every room page should answer:

- [ ] What is the room's surface story?
- [ ] What is the room's cult story?
- [ ] What is the room's deep story?
- [ ] What prop carries the clue?
- [ ] What prop carries the red herring?
- [ ] What does the room sound like?
- [ ] What does the room smell like?
- [ ] What changes at midnight?
- [ ] What is the escape route?
- [ ] What is the killer's advantage in this room?
- [ ] What is the investigator's advantage in this room?
- [ ] Is the room accessible without voice?
- [ ] Is the room readable in blackout?
- [ ] Does the room avoid gore and real-world hate symbols?
