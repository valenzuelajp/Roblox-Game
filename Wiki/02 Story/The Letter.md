# The Letter

## The prop

A cream envelope, black wax seal, no return address. The paper is heavy; the ink is typewritten; one line is handwritten in a different hand. It smells of rain and perfume.

## The text

> **BLACKWOOD LODGE**
> *Ashcombe, Vermont*
>
> 14 October 1946
>
> To the officers who closed the case:
>
> You were told the guests left. You were told the matter was finished. You were told the lodge was empty.
>
> None of that was true.
>
> The nine are still here. The house has been waiting for the ones who signed the report. Come back on the anniversary. Come back to the room where the clock stopped. Come back before the tenth guest is chosen.
>
> Bring your notes. Bring your photographs. Bring the truth, if you still have it.
>
> Do not trust the host.
>
> — V.

## The sender

**Madame Vesper.** A cult defector, fortune teller, and the keeper of the Curio Shop. She wants the cult stopped — or she wants Elias's place. The letter is genuine, but her motives are not pure.

## Why the detectives come

- Guilt: they closed a case they knew was wrong.
- Curiosity: the letter knows details that were never public.
- Fear: the letter implies they are next.
- Duty: one of them never stopped investigating.

## What the letter hides

- It does not mention the Hollow.
- It does not mention the ritual.
- It does not mention that the house needs ten souls.
- It does not mention that Vesper is a defector.
- It does not mention that the host is the survivor.

## How it becomes a clue

The letter can be used in the Case File:

- The handwriting matches a note in the Archive.
- The date is the anniversary of the 1936 rite.
- The phrase "the room where the clock stopped" points to the Foyer.
- "The tenth guest" foreshadows the ritual.
- "Do not trust the host" points to Elias.

## Variants

- If the victim is a politician, the letter includes a clipping about the victim.
- If the victim is a journalist, the letter includes a page from their notes.
- If the victim is an heiress, the letter includes a family tree.
- The letter is always typewritten; the signature is always handwritten.
