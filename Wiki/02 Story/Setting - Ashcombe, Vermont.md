# Setting - Ashcombe, Vermont

> [!note] Provisional
> This is the proposed setting from the deferred story round. It is not locked.

## The town

**Ashcombe, Vermont.** A small mill town that never recovered from the 1927 flood. Population 1,400 and falling. Old money on the ridge; timber and dairy in the valley; a main street with a diner, a bank, a sheriff's office, a church, and a movie theater that closes at 8.

- **Founded:** 1802.
- **Industry:** timber, then a wool mill, then nothing.
- **The ridge:** the Blackwood family estate and three other old houses.
- **The woods:** dense pine, old logging roads, a quarry, and a hill the locals call **the Hollow**.
- **The town's attitude:** polite, closed, and deeply uninterested in the Blackwood case.

## The year

**1947.** Post-war, pre-television, noir. Men back from the war, women who ran the town during it, rationing ending, old hierarchies cracking. The weather is unseasonably cold; the storm is the worst in a decade.

## The lodge

Blackwood Lodge sits on a ridge above the town, at the end of a private road. Three floors, a cellar, an attic, a chapel that is not on the original blueprint. Built in 1898, expanded in 1926, abandoned in 1936, reopened in 1947.

The lodge is deliberately wrong:

- Corridors that do not line up.
- A chapel with no exterior windows.
- A cellar that is larger than the foundation.
- A ballroom that can be seen from the drive but not from the garden.
- A clock that stopped at 12:00 in 1936 and started again tonight.

## The storm

- The road washes out at 8:00 PM in-game.
- The telephone line is cut.
- The radio plays static, then a voice, then static.
- The sheriff cannot reach the ridge.
- The storm is not natural; the cult has been seeding it. (Proposed; keep ambiguous.)

## Why no help is coming

1. The road is gone.
2. The phone is dead.
3. The town is afraid of the lodge.
4. The sheriff is compromised (proposed).
5. The cult wants the investigators isolated.

## The town's secrets

- The 1936 case was closed in a week.
- The nine guests were buried in a single service; the coffins were closed.
- The Blackwood family paid for the church roof, the bank, and the sheriff's campaign.
- Three townspeople remember the ninth guest walking out of the woods.
- Nobody talks about the Hollow.
