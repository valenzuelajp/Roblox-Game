## The room

### Surface story

A stone building with two bays, a 1938 sedan, a workbench, hanging tools, and a smell of oil and wet hay. The doors are heavy and the windows are painted over.

### Cult story

The sedan's trunk has a 1936 picnic blanket and a child's shoe. The workbench drawer has a chauffeur's log with nine names.

### Deep story

The carriage house is where the guests arrived. It is where the house keeps the vehicle of their arrival.

## What you see

- 1938 sedan
- Workbench
- Chauffeur's log
- Hanging tools
- Painted windows

## Clue role

Task 1 Victim (chauffeur's cap, log); Task 2 Method (tools, exhaust); Task 4 Motive (1936 ferry ticket).

## The secret

The sedan's trunk has a false floor with a spare key to the front gate. The workbench has a tunnel to the Cellar coal chute.

## Killer advantage

The carriage house is isolated. A body here is found late. The car's headlights can be left on as a lure.

## Investigator advantage

The tools are useful; the gate key is a potential escape; the tunnel is a quick route to the Cellar.

## Sound and light

**Sound:** Rain on tin, an engine ticking, a tool swinging on its hook.

**Light:** A single bulb, headlights, lightning through the painted windows.

## Midnight shift

The car engine starts by itself. The headlights turn on. The chauffeur's log gains a tenth line.

## Routes

Garden path to Conservatory, tunnel to Cellar coal chute, driveway to front gate.

## Design notes

- **Readability:** the room must be legible in blackout. Every critical clue has a text card and a lit fallback.
- **No soft-locks:** the room must have at least two routes or a key that is not behind its own lock.
- **No gore:** blood is a stain, a ribbon, or a smear.
- **Voice optional:** every interaction has a text prompt and a ping.
- **Performance:** keep unique props under 40; reuse modular props across rooms.
- **Accessibility:** no clue may depend on color alone; audio clues have subtitles.
