# Noir and Horror References

> [!info] Inspiration, not imitation
> We borrow techniques, not identities. No copyrighted character, logo, or distinctive costume should be copied.

## The tone in one sentence

**A 1940s noir detective story where the house is the suspect, the storm is the clock, and the cult is the thing the rich family paid to forget.**

## Film and television

| Reference | What we borrow | What we avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **The Maltese Falcon** (1941) | Hard-boiled dialogue, moral ambiguity, the detective as a flawed insider | Direct character copies |
| **Double Indemnity** (1944) | Voice-over, fatalism, the crime already happened | Melodrama |
| **The Third Man** (1949) | Shadows, tilted frames, a city that hides people | Post-war Vienna specifics |
| **Chinatown** (1974) | Institutional corruption, the case is bigger than the detective | Explicit content |
| **Se7en** (1995) | Staged crime scenes, dread, the killer as a curator | Gore, cruelty as spectacle |
| **The Thing** (1982) | Paranoia, isolation, the monster is among us | Body horror |
| **The Shining** (1980) | The hotel as a character, impossible architecture, repetition | Domestic abuse imagery |
| **Clue** (1985) | Multiple endings, the pleasure of reconstruction | Comedy tone |
| **Knives Out** (2019) | The detective as an outsider, the family as a suspect pool | Modern setting |
| **Ready or Not** (2019) | Old money ritual, the house as a trap | Graphic violence |
| **The Invitation** (2015) | Social unease, the dinner party as a cult | Slow-burn length |

## Literature

- **Dashiell Hammett** — clean, hard prose; the detective is not a hero.
- **Raymond Chandler** — atmosphere, simile, the city as a character.
- **Agatha Christie** — closed-circle suspects, the drawing-room reveal.
- **Shirley Jackson** — the house as a psychological presence.
- **Edgar Allan Poe** — decay, ritual, the unreliable narrator.
- **H.P. Lovecraft** — cosmic indifference, without the racism and without the purple prose.

## Games

| Game | What we borrow | What we avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **L.A. Noire** | Investigation as a verb, reading faces, case structure | Open-world scope |
| **Return of the Obra Dinn** | Deduction from evidence, the pleasure of being right | Monochrome-only accessibility |
| **Her Story** | Fragmented testimony, the player assembles the truth | FMV budget |
| **The Case of the Golden Idol** | Environmental deduction, no hand-holding | Pixel-hunting |
| **Phasmophobia** | Co-op ghost hunting, evidence tools | Jump-scare dependence |
| **Dead by Daylight** | Asymmetric horror, chase tension | Gore, licensed killers |
| **Deceit** | The reveal, trust collapse | Toxic meta |
| **Disco Elysium** | Voice, politics, the detective as a mess | Length, text density |

## Visual techniques

### Light

- **Venetian-blind shadows** across a study wall.
- **Single practical lamp** in a large room; the rest is darkness.
- **Lightning** as a freeze-frame: for one frame, the room is visible.
- **Candlelight** in the chapel; the fifth candle is black.
- **Red** only for ritual and danger; never for UI primary.

### Composition

- **Dutch angles** for unease, used sparingly.
- **Deep focus** so the player can read the room.
- **Negative space** in doorways; something is always missing.
- **Frames within frames** (mirrors, windows, doorways) to suggest surveillance.
- **Symmetry** in the chapel; **asymmetry** in the cellar.

### Materials

- Dark wood, brass, leather, velvet, marble, stained glass.
- Rain-streaked windows, wet coats, muddy boots.
- Paper: case files, ledgers, photographs, letters, newspaper clippings.
- Dust, ash, cigarette smoke, candle wax.

## Sound techniques

- **Diegetic rain** that changes with the room.
- **A clock that is always ten minutes wrong.**
- **A piano note** when the chapel opens.
- **A typewriter** that types by itself.
- **A bell** with a cracked overtone.
- **Whispers** that are directional, not random.
- **Silence** before the reveal; the rain stops.

## Narrative techniques

- **The case file as a framing device.** The story is told as a document.
- **The unreliable witness.** Every staff member has one true thing and one lie.
- **The house as a narrator.** Room changes, portraits, guest book entries.
- **The reveal as a social event.** The ritual reveals the killer to everyone.
- **The ambiguous ending.** The Hollow may be real, a story, or a delusion.

## What we avoid

- **Gore and torture.** Stylized blood only.
- **Real-world hate symbols.** The cult iconography is fictional.
- **Sexual violence.** Not in the game.
- **Harm to children.** The child's toy is a ghost story, not a depiction.
- **Mental illness as the monster.** The cult is a choice, not a diagnosis.
- **Poverty porn.** The house is rich; the town is not a joke.
- **Period-accurate bigotry played for flavor.** No.
- **Copying a distinctive character or costume.** Inspiration only.

## Mood board checklist

- [ ] Does the room read as 1940s without a caption?
- [ ] Is there one light source that tells the player where to look?
- [ ] Is there one prop that tells a story?
- [ ] Is there one shadow that feels wrong?
- [ ] Is the palette within the eight colors?
- [ ] Is the sound layer diegetic?
- [ ] Is the horror implied rather than shown?
- [ ] Would a player describe this room to a friend?
